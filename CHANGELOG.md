## [1.0.0] - 2026-10-19

Results, write-up and polish. The project is complete.

### Added

- [docs/research-report.md](docs/research-report.md): a short research report covering the
  question, data, method, results, why it probably wouldn't work live, and next steps. ([#12])
- Charts in `docs/images/`, generated by `scripts/make_charts.py` ([#12]):
  - SPY's book and trades
  - latency before and after optimisation
  - model accuracy by horizon
  - PnL before and after costs
- `latency_histogram` tool, which writes the full latency distribution of the v1 and
  current books on a real day. ([#12])
- [docs/getting-started.md](docs/getting-started.md) and
  [docs/architecture.md](docs/architecture.md). ([#12])
- CI: the C++ tests also run under AddressSanitizer and UndefinedBehaviorSanitizer, and
  Python is linted and format-checked with ruff. ([#12])

### Changed

- The README is rewritten: highlights with real numbers, an architecture diagram, results,
  a quick start, design decisions and limitations. ([#12])
- The benchmark and backtest docs are rewritten to be shorter and simpler. The long research
  doc is replaced by the report, and the full tables are in
  [docs/research/tables.md](docs/research/tables.md). ([#12])
- Benchmarks now convert TSC ticks to nanoseconds with the CPU's fixed TSC rate
  (`LOB_TSC_GHZ`). Under WSL2, calibrating against the system clock drifted by up to 9%
  between runs, because the guest clock is steered by the host. The version history was
  re-measured, with every version rebuilt from its commit and run in one session. ([#12])

## [0.6.0] - 2026-10-19

ML signal.

### Added

- `lobpy.research`, ML research on order book data ([#11]):
  - **Features:** top-level and multi-level imbalance, spread, microprice offset, order flow
    imbalance, recent trade flow, short-term volatility and momentum.
  - **Labels:** up, flat or down mid-price direction at configurable horizons.
  - **Validation:** expanding-window walk-forward with an embargo.
  - **Models**, simplest first: majority class, imbalance rule, logistic regression and
    LightGBM.
  - `PredictionTaker`, which trades model probabilities in the backtester, optionally only
    at tight spreads.
- `scripts/run_research.py`: the full study over four development days and one holdout day.
  It covers walk-forward validation, one holdout test, and backtests of the predictions at
  two thresholds. `scripts/research_tables.py` and `scripts/research_diagnose.py` produce
  the tables. ([#11])
- The research write-up (now [docs/research-report.md](docs/research-report.md)), with
  feature importance charts and raw results in `docs/research/`. ([#11])
  - **Prediction works:** LightGBM beats every baseline on holdout log-loss at 1 s, 5 s and
    30 s, and in 20 of 21 walk-forward folds.
  - **Trading doesn't:** the model is most confident when the spread is widest (median 47–56
    ticks vs 3), so trading it loses heavily.
  - **Cost-aware variant:** restricted to tradeable states, a small real edge remains at
    1–5 s, but it is smaller than the cost of crossing the spread.
- Tests for the research layer ([#11]):
  - feature formulas
  - a **no-lookahead test** that scrambles future data and checks that no earlier feature
    changes
  - walk-forward ordering and the embargo
  - models beating the baseline on a planted signal
  - the prediction strategy never using a later prediction
- `scikit-learn` and `lightgbm` added to the `dev` extra, so CI runs the research tests.
  ([#11])

## [0.5.0] - 2026-10-19

Event-driven backtester.

### Added

- `lobpy.backtest`, an event-driven backtester in four parts ([#10]):
  - **Data feed** (`EventFeed`): every book event for a symbol, in time order.
  - **Strategy** protocol: sees one `BookSnapshot` at a time and never the future.
  - **Execution simulator:**
    - **Latency:** orders and cancels reach the exchange after a configurable delay.
    - **Spread crossing:** market and IOC orders walk the visible book level by level.
    - **Queue position:** passive orders join the back of the queue and move up only as
      orders that were ahead of them execute or cancel.
    - **Passive fills:** when an order behind executes, on trade-throughs, and when the
      opposite side reaches the order's price.
    - **Fees:** per share, separately for taking and providing liquidity.
  - **Portfolio:** exact integer accounting and a position limit that counts open orders.
    From 15:55 a forced flat cancels everything and exits with IOCs at the touch.
- Metrics: net PnL split into signal, spread and fees, plus Sharpe, max drawdown, fill
  rate, turnover and order counts. ([#10])
- `Replayer.events(symbol, depth)` in C++ and Python. It returns one row per book event,
  with each affected order's queue priority and the book after the event. ([#10])
- Immediate-or-cancel limit orders (`LimitOrder(..., ioc=True)`). ([#10])
- Example strategies: buy-open/sell-close, and an order book imbalance strategy in a taker
  version and a maker version. ([#10])
- `scripts/run_backtests.py` and [docs/backtest.md](docs/backtest.md): results on SPY and
  AAPL at 10 µs, 100 µs and 1 ms latency, with assumptions and limitations. ([#10])
- Tests:
  - 19 backtester tests with hand-computed expectations, including the guide's
    buy-at-open / sell-at-close case on `testdata/sample.itch` (net −$2.60).
  - 3 C++ tests for the event feed. ([#10])

## [0.4.0] - 2026-10-17

Python bindings.

### Added

- `lobpy.Replayer`, pybind11 bindings to a new C++ `lob::Replayer` that replays an ITCH file
  for chosen symbols. ([#9])
  - `snapshots(interval_ns, depth, start_ns, end_ns)` samples the top N levels on a fixed
    time grid.
  - `trades()` returns every execution with its aggressor side, match number and kind.
  - Results are dicts of **zero-copy NumPy arrays**: C++ fills preallocated buffers, and a
    capsule hands their ownership to NumPy.
  - The GIL is released during the replay.
  - Requests whose output would exceed 4 GiB are rejected with a clear error.
- `lobpy.snapshots_frame` and `lobpy.trades_frame`, which convert results to pandas
  DataFrames with dollar prices, mid and spread. ([#9])
- `testdata/sample.itch`, a hand-built ITCH day with documented expected books and trades,
  plus its generator `scripts/make_sample_itch.py`. The C++ and Python tests assert the same
  values against it. ([#9])
- Notebook `notebooks/01_replay_snapshots.ipynb`. It replays the BX day from Python, charts
  SPY's mid and spread, and previews the order book imbalance signal. ([#9])

### Changed

- The Python package now builds with scikit-build-core. `pip install .` compiles the C++
  engine and the extension through the existing CMake file, with tests, tools and benchmarks
  turned off. ([#9])

## [0.3.0] - 2026-10-17

Order book, matching engine and benchmarks.

### Added

- `lob::OrderBook`, a single-instrument limit order book with price-time priority. It
  supports add, reduce, remove, cancel-replace (which loses time priority), top of book,
  depth, and front-of-queue access for matching. ([#5])
- `lob::BookBuilder`, an ITCH `parse()` handler that rebuilds a book per stock locate and
  counts anomalies instead of failing. ([#5])
- `book_replay` tool. It replays a full day and reports anomalies, locked or crossed books
  by session, top of book at the 16:00 close, and a top-of-book digest over every update.
  On the 30 July 2019 BX day it finds 0 anomalies, no locked or crossed books, and closing
  quotes that match that day's real prices. ([#5], [#7])
- `lob::MatchingEngine`, with price-time priority and limit, market, IOC and cancel orders.
  Each trade emits a `Fill` event (price, quantity, aggressor side, both order ids) through
  a compile-time callback. Self-trade prevention can cancel either the incoming or the
  resting order. ([#6])
- `lob::OrderIndex`, an open-addressing hash map with linear probing, Fibonacci hashing and
  backward-shift deletion. ([#8])
- `lob_bench`, a Google Benchmark suite:
  - Per-operation p50 / p99 / p99.9 latency for add, cancel and match, read with `rdtsc`
    into a one-cycle histogram.
  - Latency of every book update in a real ITCH day, by message type.
  - Full-day replay throughput.
  - Same-run comparisons against the original implementation. ([#7], [#8])
- `-DLOB_NATIVE=ON` CMake option for `-march=native` builds. ([#7])
- `scripts/bench_summary.py`, which turns benchmark JSON into Markdown tables. ([#7])
- Tests:
  - Edge cases for the matching engine.
  - A differential test of the matching engine against a brute-force model over 100,000
    random orders.
  - A differential test of `OrderBook` against the frozen original implementation.
  - Unit and churn tests for `OrderIndex`. ([#5], [#6], [#8])

### Changed

- The order book was optimised in three benchmarked steps, each profiled with `perf`:
  - **Price levels:** flat sorted vectors instead of `std::map`.
  - **Orders:** a pooled order store with intrusive per-level queues, instead of a
    `std::list` per level.
  - **Order index:** open addressing instead of `std::unordered_map`.

  Together, in the same run: full-day replay is 2.4× faster (12.5 to 30.3 M msg/s), and
  real-data add p99 latency fell from 210 to 120 ns. See
  [docs/benchmarks.md](docs/benchmarks.md). ([#8])

## [0.2.0] - 2026-10-17

ITCH 5.0 market data parser.

### Added

- ITCH 5.0 message definitions and decoders (`lob/itch.hpp`) for System Event, Stock
  Directory, Add Order (with and without MPID), Order Executed (with and without price), Order
  Cancel, Order Delete, Order Replace, Trade, Cross Trade and Broken Trade. Byte offsets are
  checked against the official specification. Prices are kept as integers with 4 implied
  decimals. ([#2])
- Stream parser `lob::itch::parse()` (`lob/itch_parser.hpp`). The handler is a template
  parameter, so callbacks are resolved at compile time: no virtual calls and no allocation per
  message. Message lengths are checked for all 23 ITCH 5.0 types, and parsing stops with an
  error code on truncated or malformed input. ([#2])
- `lob::MappedFile`, a read-only, memory-mapped view of a file (`mmap` with
  `MADV_SEQUENTIAL`). ([#2])
- `itch_stats` command-line tool. It reports message counts per type, checks that
  timestamps never go backwards and that the day runs from start to end of messages, and
  times the parser with and without full field decoding. ([#2])
- Parser benchmark on a full NASDAQ BX day (28.7M messages, 837 MB): 0.18 s with full field
  decoding, 6.3 ns per message. See [docs/benchmarks.md](docs/benchmarks.md). ([#2])
- 30 GoogleTest unit tests covering every decoder, the parser's handling of message
  boundaries and errors, and `MappedFile`. ([#2])
- CI check that C++ sources match `clang-format`. ([#2])
- This changelog. ([#3])

### Changed

- Compiler warnings raised to `-Wall -Wextra -Wpedantic -Wshadow -Wconversion
  -Wsign-conversion` for all project targets. ([#2])
- Minimum CMake version raised to 3.25, so GoogleTest's headers are treated as system headers
  and excluded from the stricter warnings. ([#2])

### Removed

- Placeholder `lob::ItchParser` class, replaced by `lob::itch::parse()`. ([#2])
